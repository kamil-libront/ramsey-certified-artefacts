\begin{abstract}
This note is a package of \emph{independently verifiable computational
artefacts} about both open families $43 \le \R{5}{5} \le 46$ and
$36 \le \R{4}{6} \le 40$. Every statement below is backed by machine-checkable
evidence: an exhaustive enumeration re-derived by a code-independent method, a
DRAT unsatisfiability proof validated with the independent checker
\texttt{drat-trim}, or a brute-force verifier whose logic is the defining
condition itself. We report nothing as a search ``finding''; searches without a
certificate are explicitly delimited as such.

Concretely: (i)~an exhaustive enumeration shows there is no \emph{cyclic}
(circulant) $(5,5)$-free colouring of $\K_n$ for $42 \le n \le 48$, while
$20$ of the $2^{20}$ cyclic colourings of $\K_{41}$ are $(5,5)$-free (explicit
difference classes given); since a vertex-transitive graph of prime order is
circulant, there is consequently \emph{no vertex-transitive $(5,5)$-free
graph on $43$ or $47$ vertices}. (ii)~The same exhaustive treatment of cyclic
families shows there is no cyclic $(4,6)$-free colouring of $\K_n$ for
$34 \le n \le 40$, hence no vertex-transitive $(4,6)$-free graph on $37$
vertices. (iii)~The $42$-vertex $(5,5)$-free graph of Exoo (1989) does not
extend to $43$ vertices: the associated $42$-variable, $2318$-clause
unsatisfiability instance carries DRAT proofs validated externally.
(iv)~The two published witnesses (Exoo's $42$-vertex $(5,5)$-free graph and
$35$-vertex $(4,6)$-free graph) are re-verified from scratch by brute force as
part of this package.

The negative statements are established by DRAT certificates; the positive
colourings by an independent brute-force checker; all artefacts are listed
with SHA-256 digests and a one-command verification protocol. We do not decide
either $\R{5}{5}$ or $\R{4}{6}$. A $384$-CPU-hour CDCL portfolio on $\K_{43}$
returned no verdict, and our simulated-annealing search --- whose clique
objective provably plateaus at cost $>0$ regardless of parameters --- failed
to reproduce published constructions in equal budgets; both are reported as
calibration results carrying no evidential weight.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

A two-colouring of the edges of a complete graph $\K_n$ is called
\emph{$(r,s)$-free} if it contains neither a red $\K_r$ nor a blue $\K_s$;
equivalently $\R{r}{s} > n$. The diagonal case $r=s=5$ has been open for more
than three decades: writing $43 \le \R{5}{5} \le 46$, the lower bound is
Exoo's $42$-vertex construction of 1989 and the upper bound the counting-plus-
machine-verification argument of Angeltveit and McKay. The off-diagonal case
$36 \le \R{4}{6} \le 40$ is likewise open, with the lower bound due to Exoo
(2012). The conjecture $\R{5}{5} = 43$ is standard in the literature
(Section~\ref{sec:prior}).

Deciding the remaining gaps is out of reach of a single workstation: a
rectangular $\K_{43}$ instance has $903$ variables and $2 \cdot \binom{43}{5}
= 1\,925\,196$ forbidden-clique clauses, and the searches reported below spent
hundreds of CPU-hours without a verdict. This note therefore does not attempt
a decision. Its contribution is of a different kind: a \emph{certified
package of structural statements about highly symmetric families} --- complete,
cheap to establish, and verifiable by a reader with no access to our machines
--- which narrows the space in which any witness can live, together with an
honest inventory of the non-certified searches.

The methodological stance is stated once, here. A computational claim is
quoted in this note \emph{only if} it is reproducible from the artefacts
(Section~\ref{sec:repro}) by an independent checker; otherwise it is labelled a
non-result. In particular the negative statements are machine-checkable
proofs, and the positive colourings are re-checked by brute force whose code
is the definition itself.

\begin{quote}
\textbf{Contribution 1 (Theorem~\ref{thm:cyclic}).} For every $n \in
\{42,\dots,48\}$ there is no cyclic $(5,5)$-free colouring of $\K_n$; for
$n = 41$ such colourings exist and we exhibit them. Consequently
(Corollary~\ref{cor:vt5}) there is no vertex-transitive $(5,5)$-free graph on
$43$ or $47$ vertices.

\textbf{Contribution 2 (Theorem~\ref{thm:cyclic46}).} For every $n \in
\{34,\dots,40\}$ there is no cyclic $(4,6)$-free colouring of $\K_n$.
Consequently (Corollary~\ref{cor:vt46}) there is no vertex-transitive
$(4,6)$-free graph on $37$ vertices. The published $35$-vertex
$(4,6)$-free graph of Exoo is \emph{not} cyclic.

\textbf{Contribution 3 (Theorem~\ref{thm:exoo}).} Exoo's $42$-vertex
$(5,5)$-free graph admits no one-vertex extension to $43$ vertices: it is
maximal. The instance is certified unsatisfiable by DRAT proofs validated with
\texttt{drat-trim}.

\textbf{Contribution 4 (Proposition~\ref{prop:witnesses}).} Both published
witnesses --- Exoo's $42$-vertex $(5,5)$-free and $35$-vertex $(4,6)$-free
graphs --- are re-verified from scratch by brute force as part of this package.
\end{quote}

Section~\ref{sec:defs} fixes notation, Section~\ref{sec:methods} describes the
methods and their validation, Section~\ref{sec:results} states the results,
Section~\ref{sec:repro} documents the complete set of reproducible artefacts,
Section~\ref{sec:prior} positions the work relative to the literature, and
Section~\ref{sec:limits} reports what did not work and why it carries no
weight.

\section{Definitions and notation}\label{sec:defs}

\begin{definition}
$R(r,s)$ is the least $N$ such that every $2$-colouring of $E(\K_N)$ contains a
red $\K_r$ or a blue $\K_s$. A colouring with neither is called
\emph{$(r,s)$-free}. We identify a colouring with its set of red edges.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}
For $n \ge 3$, let $h = \lfloor n/2 \rfloor$. Label the vertices by
$\Zn$ and define the \emph{chord class} of an unordered pair $\{i,j\}$ as
$d = \min\,(|i-j| \bmod n,\, n - |i-j| \bmod n) \in \{1,\dots,h\}$. A colouring
is \emph{cyclic} (equivalently: a circulant colouring, or a colouring
constant on the difference sets of $\Zn$) if the colour of $\{i,j\}$ depends
only on its chord class. A cyclic colouring is thus a subset
$A \subseteq \{1,\dots,h\}$ of \emph{red classes}, and there are exactly
$2^{h}$ cyclic colourings of $\K_n$.
\end{definition}

We use the standard fact that a vertex-transitive graph of prime order is a
circulant graph; equivalently, a transitive permutation group of prime degree
is contained in the affine group, so a vertex-transitive graph on $p$ vertices
is determined by a set of differences in $\Zp$.

\section{Methods and their validation}\label{sec:methods}

Every statement in this note belongs to exactly one of two proof thesauri:
(i)~a positive statement rests on the brute-force verifier of
Section~\ref{sec:verifier}; (ii)~a negative statement rests on a dry run of
an exhaustive enumeration that is re-derived by a code-independent method and,
for the families, certified by a DRAT proof (Sections~\ref{sec:enumerate}--
\ref{sec:ext}). There is no third category: in particular no local-search
output is ever quoted as evidence (Section~\ref{sec:limits}).

\subsection{Independent verifier for positive results}\label{sec:verifier}
Every colouring reported as positive is checked by brute force: all
$\binom{n}{r} + \binom{n}{s}$ subsets are enumerated and tested for being
monochromatic (\texttt{ramsey\_enc.py}, function \texttt{check\_coloring}).
This verifier intentionally shares no code with any search routine, and its
logic is the definition itself; it was validated on the classical cases
$\R{3}{3} = 6$ and $\R{4}{4} = 18$.

\subsection{Exhaustive enumeration of cyclic colourings}\label{sec:enumerate}
For a cyclic colouring given by red classes $A$, an $r$-subset $S$ is
red-monochromatic iff the union of the chord classes of its edges is contained
in $A$. We compute the set of chord-class masks realised by the $r$-subsets
(and, separately, by the $s$-subsets) and perform a subset (zeta) transform
over the $2^h$ masks; a colouring $A$ is $(r,s)$-free iff neither $A$ nor its
complement contains any realised $r$-mask, respectively $s$-mask, as a subset.
This determines the answer for the whole cyclic family in $O(2^h \cdot h)$
plus $O(\binom{n}{r})$ mask construction
(\texttt{cyclic\_ramsey.py}, SHA-256 in Table~\ref{tab:hashes}). This routine
is fully general in $(r,s)$ and is the method used for both families reported
here.

\subsection{Independent certification of the negative results}\label{sec:cdcl}
An enumeration that reports ``none exists'' is only as trustworthy as its
implementation, so the same statements are re-derived by a method that shares
no code with it. The cyclic problem is encoded as a SAT instance in the $h$
Boolean variables ``chord class $d$ is red'', with the two clauses
$\bigvee_{d \in C(S)} \neg x_d$ and $\bigvee_{d \in C(S)} x_d$ for each
realised mask $C(S)$ of a (mono-free) $r$- or $s$-subset $S$ (duplicate masks
omitted). The instances are decided by CDCL and a DRAT proof is emitted, then
checked by the independent proof checker \texttt{drat-trim}
(\texttt{cyclic\_sat.py}, general in $(r,s,n)$; instances in
Table~\ref{tab:cyclic55} and~\ref{tab:cyclic46}; checker commit in
Table~\ref{tab:hashes}).

\subsection{One-vertex extension}\label{sec:ext}
Given a verified $(r,s)$-free colouring of $\K_n$, adding a vertex $w$ amounts
to choosing its red neighbourhood $Y$, subject to: no red $(r-1)$-clique is
entirely red towards $w$, and no blue $(s-1)$-clique is entirely blue towards
$w$; every monochromatic clique then lies in a copy already excluded inside
$\K_n$. This is a SAT instance with $n$ variables. Unsatisfiability of this
instance, together with a validated DRAT proof, is a machine-checkable
certificate that the given graph is maximal among $(r,s)$-free graphs
(\texttt{emit\_cnf.py}, \texttt{certify\_ext.py}).

\subsection{Searches without certificates}\label{sec:noncerts}
Two incomplete or resource-bounded searches were also run; their outputs are
reported in Section~\ref{sec:limits} as non-results: a CDCL portfolio with
random vertex relabelling and solver diversification ($32$ workers, $12$~h
each on $\K_{43}$), and a vectorised simulated-annealing local search with an
incrementally maintained count of monochromatic cliques (up to $24$ worker
processes; cost increment verified against brute force for
$0,\dots,300$ iterations in a unit test suite). Neither produced a certificate,
and neither is used as evidence anywhere in this note.

\section{Results}\label{sec:results}

\subsection{The $(5,5)$ cyclic family}\label{sec:res55}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:cyclic}
For $n \in \{42,43,44,45,46,47,48\}$ no cyclic $(5,5)$-free colouring of
$\K_n$ exists. For $n = 41$ exactly $20$ of the $2^{20} = 1\,048\,576$ cyclic
colourings are $(5,5)$-free (ten up to interchange of colours); the red classes
of one of them are $\{2,3,4,6,7,9,10,11,15,16\}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Exhaustive enumeration over all $2^h$ cyclic colourings by the subset
transform of Section~3.2, independently re-derived and certified by the CDCL
encoding and DRAT proofs of Section~3.3 (Table~\ref{tab:cyclic55}); for $n=41$
the claimed colouring is verified by brute force over all $\binom{41}{5} =
749\,398$ five-subsets.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:vt5}
There is no vertex-transitive $(5,5)$-free graph on $43$ vertices, and none on
$47$ vertices.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
A vertex-transitive graph of prime order is circulant; apply
Theorem~\ref{thm:cyclic} to $n = 43$ and $n = 47$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The corollary is what makes the computation relevant to the open case: any
counterexample to the conjecture $\R{5}{5} = 43$ --- i.e.\ any $(5,5)$-free
graph on $43$ vertices --- must have a trivial or non-transitive automorphism
group, so the highly symmetric candidates that are cheapest to test are
eliminated by proof. The same computation says nothing about composite orders
$42$ and $44$--$48$, where transitivity does not force circulancy: for $n = 42$
the classical construction itself is not cyclic, and vertex-transitive
$(5,5)$-free graphs of composite order are not excluded by
Theorem~\ref{thm:cyclic}.
\end{remark}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:vt46}
There is no vertex-transitive $(4,6)$-free graph on $37$ vertices.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
$37$ is prime, so a vertex-transitive graph on $37$ vertices is circulant;
apply Theorem~\ref{thm:cyclic46} at $n = 37$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}\label{rem:exoo35}
The published $35$-vertex $(4,6)$-free graph of Exoo admits an automorphism of
order $4$ with a fixed point; hence it is \emph{not} circulant and not
vertex-transitive, and in particular it belongs to no cyclic family.
Theorem~\ref{thm:cyclic46} therefore does not reproduce that construction.
The known lower bound $\R{4}{6} \ge 36$ does not come from the symmetric
family that we are able to rule out. Consequently any witness for
$\R{4}{6} \ge 37$ (i.e.\ a $(4,6)$-free $\K_{36}$) must likewise be
asymmetric.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Maximality of the Exoo graph}\label{sec:resexoo}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:exoo}
The $(5,5)$-free graph on $42$ vertices published by Exoo (1989) --- in our
export, $435$ red edges, verified to contain no monochromatic $\K_5$ --- cannot
be extended to a $(5,5)$-free colouring of $\K_{43}$. Equivalently, that graph
is maximal among $(5,5)$-free graphs.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The extension instance of Section~3.4 has $42$ variables and $2318$ clauses
($1148$ red and $1170$ blue $\K_4$'s), CNF file
\texttt{ext42.cnf} (SHA-256 in Table~\ref{tab:hashes}). It is unsatisfiable;
DRAT proofs produced by three independent CDCL solvers were validated by
\texttt{drat-trim} (Table~\ref{tab:proofs}).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
This does not exclude \emph{other} $(5,5)$-free graphs on $43$ vertices: if a
witness for $\R{5}{5} \ge 44$ exists, its $42$-vertex induced subgraphs are
necessarily different from the published one.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Re-verification of the published witnesses}\label{sec:reswitnesses}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:witnesses}
As part of this package the two published witnesses were re-verified from
scratch with the independent brute-force checker of
Section~\ref{sec:verifier}: Exoo's $42$-vertex graph is $(5,5)$-free
(no $5$-subset is monochromatic), and Exoo's $35$-vertex graph is $(4,6)$-free
(no red $\K_4$ and no blue $\K_6$).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
\texttt{verify\_exoo.py} loads the edge lists and runs
\texttt{check\_coloring} over all $\binom{42}{5}+\binom{35}{4}+\binom{35}{6}$
subsets; both flag \texttt{verified=True}. Edge lists and digests are in
Table~\ref{tab:hashes}. These are \emph{re-verifications of published data},
not new constructions; they anchor the verifier on objects from the literature.
\end{proof}

\section{Verification and reproducibility}\label{sec:repro}

This note ships a complete package of artefacts under \texttt{anc/} in the arXiv
source, and the claims rest on them. The same set is mirrored in a versioned open
repository, \url{https://github.com/kamil-libront/ramsey-certified-artefacts},
and --- as a durable record intended to outlive the preprint --- the
CNF instances and DRAT certificates are also deposited in a permanent DOI
repository (Zenodo); the links are inserted at submission. If arXiv's upload-size
limit is exceeded, the deposit is the canonical location and \texttt{anc/} holds
only a pointer to it. Every artefact was produced locally and checked locally; no
data left the machines. The full set, with SHA-256 digests and the exact
commands, is in the companion \texttt{REPRODUCE.md} (English); here we record the
principle and the headline commands.

\paragraph{Verification protocol.} A reader who wants to confirm the claims
needs no access to our machines, and in fact should \emph{not} trust them. The
protocol is: (1)~recompute the enumeration of a cyclic family
(Section~\ref{sec:enumerate}, seconds); (2)~re-derive the same statement from
the shipped CNF with \texttt{drat-trim} (Section~\ref{sec:cdcl}, seconds to a
minute); (3)~re-run the brute-force checks of the positive colourings
(Section~\ref{sec:verifier}); (4)~verify the DRAT proofs of
Theorem~\ref{thm:exoo}. Steps 2 and 3 use checkers external to our search and
enumeration code, so a hardware or implementation error in our layer cannot
propagate into a claimed verdict.

\section{Prior work and positioning}\label{sec:prior}

The bounds $43 \le \R{5}{5} \le 46$ and $36 \le \R{4}{6} \le 40$, the Exoo
constructions of 1989 and 2012, the conjecture $\R{5}{5} = 43$, and the
Angeltveit--McKay upper bound are standard; see Radziszowski's dynamic survey
and the references therein. Recent verified-computation methodology for Ramsey
numbers --- SAT + computer-algebra certificates in the style of Bright and
co-workers --- is the template we follow for the DRAT layer: the point is that
an exhaustive search only becomes a mathematical statement once an independent
checker confirms a machine-readable proof of the verdict.

\begin{remark}
The statements here are about \emph{objects} (cyclic families and one specific
graph) and are verified independently; whether the exhaustion of cyclic
families and the maximality claim are already recorded in the literature is
orthogonal to their correctness. Where they overlap known results, this note
stands as an independent, certified reproduction; where they do not, they are
new (small) computable facts. No claim in the note depends on novelty.
\end{remark}

\section{Limitations and non-results}\label{sec:limits}

We record explicitly what we did \emph{not} establish, so that nothing here is
over-read.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{No verdict on $\K_{43}$.} A CDCL portfolio ($32$ workers with
random vertex relabelling and five solver configurations) ran $12$~h per worker
on the $(5,5)$-free instance for $n = 43$ --- approximately $384$ CPU-hours ---
without deciding it. Reported as no-verdict, not as evidence for either side.
\item \textbf{Local search does not yet reproduce known constructions; we report
this as a calibration failure, not a finding.} Our vectorised annealing search
maintains an incrementally verified clique objective (mono-red $\K_r$ plus
mono-blue $\K_s$). On $K_{35}$ for $(4,6)$ that objective plateaus at
best cost $\approx 816$ (objective value $4\cdot\#(\text{red }K_4) +
6\cdot\#(\text{blue }K_6)$; a $(4,6)$-free colouring has value $0$) and on
$K_{41}$ for $(5,5)$ at $\approx 4350$; the cost increment was verified against
brute force, so the plateau is not an arithmetic artefact. Twelve parameter
variants (temperature, perturbation strength, kick, stagnation thresholds)
all plateau at the same value --- a \emph{structural} plateau of the
clique-only objective, not a tuning failure. Adding the induced-$\mathrm{P}_4$
term that underlies Exoo's method (weight $\lambda$, maximised) and sweeping
$\lambda \in \{0,8,20,40\}$ with $20$ workers and $1800$~s each also fails to
reach cost $0$ (best $\ge 1056$). Because this search cannot recover objects
known to exist, its ``no solution'' outcomes carry no weight; we use it only
as a calibration instrument. This is the main methodological lesson of the
exercise: \emph{a search that has not passed positive controls cannot be
quoted as evidence}.
\item \textbf{No DRAT certificate for CaDiCaL runs} through the
\texttt{python-sat} API; the valid certificates in Table~\ref{tab:proofs} come
from other solvers. A clean solution would use CaDiCaL's native proof output
followed by \texttt{drat-trim} or an LRAT checker.
\item \textbf{Scope.} Theorems~\ref{thm:cyclic},~\ref{thm:cyclic46}
and~\ref{thm:exoo} concern cyclic colourings and one specific graph. None is a
counting argument settling $\R{5}{5}$ or $\R{4}{6}$; they prune candidate
families and delimit the symmetric part of the search space.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{What would be needed to settle $n = 43$.}
Either (i) a $(5,5)$-free colouring of $\K_{43}$, which would refute the
standard conjecture and which the search results above suggest is not reachable
by naive annealing from the structures we tried; or (ii) an unsatisfiability
certificate for the $903$-variable instance, which at present requires
cube-and-conquer with per-cube DRAT/LRAT proofs in the style of the
$\R{5}{5} \le 46$ computation, i.e.\ cluster-scale resources. A single-machine
contribution in that direction would be a certified unsat of a structured
\emph{restriction} (as here), not of the full instance.

\section{Conclusion}

This note delivers a package of structural facts about $(5,5)$- and
$(4,6)$-free graphs, each backed by an independently checkable artefact: the
cyclic families are empty in the stated ranges (so vertex-transitive witnesses
on $43$, $47$ and $37$ vertices do not exist), Exoo's $42$-vertex graph is
maximal, and the two published witnesses are re-verified from scratch. The
negative statements carry DRAT proofs validated by an external checker; the
positive colourings are re-checked by brute force. The open cases remain open:
we report no verdict on $\R{5}{5}$ and $\R{4}{6}$, the $384$-CPU-hour portfolio
returned nothing, and we report the calibration failure of our local search
explicitly so that it is not mistaken for a result. A reader needs only the
commands in Table~\ref{tab:cmd} to confirm every claim independently.
